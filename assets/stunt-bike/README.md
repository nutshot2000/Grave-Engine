# Ignition 450: physical material assemblies

The motorcycle is ten physical assemblies: frame, engine, fork/handlebars, tank/radiator, tail, front mudguard, exhaust, seat, rear wheel and front wheel. Each has its own body, material-cell shape and attachment. Detached parts remain physical objects and retain their momentum. All bike parts are drawn by the engine material renderer from the start, including textures, heat, burning and missing cells. Blender beauty images are reference assets, not overlays in the game.

Tyres and grips use engine rubber, structural parts and panels use iron, and gold hardware uses brass. Wheel rims contain iron while the tyre circumference is rubber. Rubber friction, bounce and burning come from materials/rubber.txt; iron density, thermal conduction, melting and toughness come from materials/iron.txt. These are real simulation materials.

Blender source: Ignition-450.blend. Rendered parts and material masks are generated by tools/export_physical_bike.py. parts.json lists the cell material counts; parts.tsv connects the rendered assemblies to the game; physical-bike.txt is the reusable scene body/joint data. The course embeds that body data in scenes/stunt-yard.txt.

Engine additions: `depth 0.02` after a body sets its physical thickness in metres (default remains 1). This scales mass and buoyancy without changing material density. `break 500` on a joint sets its linear failure load in kN. Omit `break` for the previous unbreakable behaviour. Thickness and joint limits survive saves, resets, undo snapshots and object fragmentation. Welded vehicle parts suppress internal contacts; detached groups resume normal collision filtering. Balance changes apply coherently to the connected assembly.

Game controls: Space starts, D/right accelerates, A/left reverses, Q/E leans, R resets. C runs a deliberate high-speed wall impact. It sets the assembled bike's initial speed; damage and detachment are caused by the actual wall collision, not a scripted disassembly.

Verification: all 112 tests passed, covering course completion and reset, tyre material identity and ignition, a wall crash leaving detached physical parts, joint load failure and save/load, thickness/mass and fragmentation, plus the original engine suite. The live game was observed completing the course. The course's fire clearance and splash section were adjusted for the heavier material-based assembly.

Limit: this is 2D cell geometry, not exact 3D mesh collision. The rider is still presentation artwork rather than an independent physical ragdoll. Attachment thresholds are gameplay tuning, not real motorcycle engineering data.
