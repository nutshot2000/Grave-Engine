# Wreck Run — material stunt course

Open Play-Stunt-Yard.cmd. SPACE starts, D/right accelerates, A/left reverses, Q/E leans, S/down brakes. R rebuilds the bike at the last checkpoint; Backspace starts the whole course again.

The 360-metre route now has distinct sections rather than repeated ramps:

- Burning rubber, wood and oil in the scrapyard jump. Ignites as you approach.
- Two rolling sand hills over breakable sandstone. Loose sand resists the tyres.
- A small kicker before a low anchored steel gantry: brake and roll over it. Full throttle launches the rider into the beam; helmet contact with solid scenery causes a crash.
- Wet sand and water that reduce speed, followed by a recovery stretch.
- A demolition jump: passing the charge ignites actual gunpowder below/behind the bike. The engine produces the blast and damages the timber structure.
- A wooden bridge above a water trench, followed by gravel hills.
- A grippy climb into a slippery icy descent. Brakes have much less effect on ice.
- A burning scaffold over lava, then another sand climb and a second demolition charge.
- Four checkpoints reduce the cost of crashing. Restart rebuilds the course, keeps awarded gates, and disables already-passed ignition cues.

The level is ordinary scene data generated by tools/make_stunt_course.py. Proximity ignition cues are in scenes/stunt-yard-events.txt. They add heat once; they do not fake fire or explosion damage. Sandstone is a reusable material that breaks into sand. The bike has slightly more low-speed motor torque for climbs.

Tyre resistance and braking are game controls sampled from real nearby material cells; they are not a complete engine-wide tyre deformation or traction model. Physics::damp_horizontal changes assembly velocity without rebuilding bodies or losing joints. Brakes only act when the tyres detect ground; ice gets weaker braking. The rider still has no ragdoll, and stage scores still use passage gates rather than judged tricks. No sound yet.

The camera smoothly widens from 220 to 280 cells as horizontal speed increases, then eases back in more slowly when braking. Zoom is limited and the view stays inside the world boundaries.
