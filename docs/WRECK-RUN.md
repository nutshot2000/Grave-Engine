# Wreck Run — material stunt course

Open Play-Stunt-Yard.cmd (it builds the latest version first). Press 1 for Wreck Run (2 is Mountain Run, see the README), then SPACE. Trials controls: W/up throttle, S/down brake (hold it when stopped to back up), A/left lean back, D/right lean forward (Q/E work too). R rebuilds the bike at the last checkpoint; Backspace starts the whole course again.

The 360-metre route now has distinct sections rather than repeated ramps:

- Burning rubber, wood and oil in the scrapyard jump. Ignites as you approach.
- Two rolling sand hills over breakable sandstone. Loose sand resists the tyres.
- A small kicker before a low anchored steel gantry: brake and roll over it. Full throttle launches the rider into the beam; helmet contact with solid scenery causes a crash.
- Wet sand and water that reduce speed, followed by a recovery stretch.
- A demolition jump: passing the charge ignites actual gunpowder below/behind the bike. The engine produces the blast and damages the timber structure.
- A wooden bridge above a water trench, followed by gravel hills.
- A grippy climb into a slippery icy descent. Brakes have much less effect on ice.
- A burning scaffold over lava, then another sand climb and a second demolition charge.
- Four checkpoints reduce the cost of crashing. Restart rebuilds the course, keeps awarded gates, and disables already-passed ignition cues.

The level is ordinary scene data generated by tools/make_stunt_course.py, built from the engine's level shapes (`hill`, `slope`, `cover`). The fuses and checkpoints are engine triggers in the scene (`trigger ... by frame : heat circle ... ; message ...`, `... : checkpoint X Y`). Fuses set real cells hot once; the material rules make the fire and explosions. Sandstone is a reusable material that breaks into sand. The bike has slightly more low-speed motor torque for climbs.

Tyre resistance, brakes and balance are engine vehicle controls: each material's `rolling_resistance` slows the bike where the tyres actually touch it, the brakes lock the wheels so stopping depends on real grip (weak on ice, none in the air), and the scene's `upright frame strength 70 air 0.15 heavy 1.6` is the trials style: heavy, following the slope on the ground, and lean rotates the bike in the air. On sand, gravel, mud and water the rear tyre throws a roost of real cells behind the bike (they land again, so the dunes slowly get scuffed). The rider still has no ragdoll, and stage scores still use passage gates rather than judged tricks. No sound yet.

The camera smoothly widens from 220 to 340 cells as horizontal speed increases, then eases back in more slowly when braking. Zoom is limited and the view stays inside the world boundaries.
